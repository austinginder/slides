Bug bounty programs are getting slaughtered by AI submissions. So what actually makes a submission real?

Prove

A finding is not a validation.

AI will create noise. If you want to prove it, you have to tell it to prove it.

Finding

Looks exploitable in the source. Could be nothing.

→
Validation

It fired. Or it didn't. Now you know.

Prove

Use AI to prove AI.

Find the bugs. Then spin up a real environment and make it fire. Or kill the claim.

The loop

1. Look through this codebase. Find the security bugs.

2. Spin up a local site. Prove them, or disprove them.

3. Write the walkthrough. This command, then this, then you have access you shouldn't.

Anyone with a project should start here. Audit it. Then ask it to fix what it finds.

Core

My cyber AI couldn't reproduce it either.

Until I cheated and fed it the published exploit. Then two or three days later, the researcher posted the method.

What I thought

A really smart security person, combined with crazy AI. No way a person found that alone.

What they published

GPT Sol, ultra mode. Recursively keep trying. Direct it at unauthorized criticals. Chain the lows until they get worse.

Ante

I tried to copy it.
Burned weeks of Sol. Struck out.

Weekly usage resets. Three or four weeks burned in one week. Searching too many plugins, not enough time on one.

What failed

Copy the famous hunt. Wander. No target. A chaining sort of thing, nothing that paid.

What I changed

Up the ante. Two big targets. Opus 4.8. Chain hunt plus deep dive. As many subagents as you need. Run as long as you need.

Release

Put the audit in the release cycle.

Not once. Every version. Before v1. Before 1.1. Before you ship.

What people hope
  • AI writes the code, so it's secure
  • One big audit, then we're done
  • The model already knows the whole app
What actually happens
  • 10,000 lines. Not all in the context window.
  • New features still create holes.
  • If you don't check, someone else will. Or they won't tell you.
